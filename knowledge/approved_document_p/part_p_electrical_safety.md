# Part P Electrical Safety – Scope and Application – Chunk 1

**Document:** Approved Document P: Electrical Safety – Dwellings (2013 edition)  
**Regulation:** Building Regulations Part P  
**Scope:** Dwellings, common parts of residential buildings, and associated land/outbuildings  
**Duty Holders:** Designers, Installers, Building Owners

Part P requires electrical installations in dwellings to be designed and installed to protect persons from fire or injury. The 2013 edition reduces the range of notifiable work and allows third-party certification by registered certifiers as an alternative to building control approval.

Legal Requirement P1 states that reasonable provision shall be made in the design and installation of electrical installations to protect persons operating, maintaining or altering the installations from fire or injury.

Limits on application: Applies only to electrical installations intended to operate at low or extra-low voltage that are in or attached to a dwelling, in the common parts of a building serving one or more dwellings (excluding power supplies to lifts), in a building receiving electricity from a source located within or shared with a dwelling, or in a garden or on land associated with a dwelling where electricity is from a source within or shared with the dwelling.

The scope covers dwelling-houses, flats, and attached installations including external lighting, air conditioning, PV panels, and garden electrics. It includes outbuildings like sheds, detached garages, and domestic greenhouses sharing electricity with a dwelling. Common access areas of blocks of flats such as corridors and staircases are covered, as are shared amenities including laundries, kitchens, and gymnasiums.

# Part P Electrical Safety – Notifiable Work Requirements – Chunk 2

Common access areas of blocks of flats and shared amenities are covered by Part P, as are business premises connected to the same meter as a dwelling (e.g., shop below a flat). Business premises with separate metering from dwellings and power supplies to lifts in blocks of flats are not covered, though lift installations in single dwellings are included. **Duty Holders:** Electricians, Installers, Building Control Bodies

Building notice or full plans are required for three specific types of work: installation of a new circuit, replacement of a consumer unit, or additions/alterations to existing circuits in a special location.

Special locations include rooms containing a bath or shower. This encompasses the space extending vertically from floor level to 2.25m (or height of shower head if higher), and horizontally 0.6m from the bath or shower tray edge (or 1.2m from the center of the shower head if no tray). Rooms containing swimming pools or sauna heaters are also classified as special locations. Additionally, socket-outlets should not be located within 3m of a bath tub or shower tray for safety.

The following do not require building notice but must still comply with BS 7671: additions and alterations to existing installations outside special locations; replacements, repairs, and maintenance anywhere; installing fixed electrical equipment like built-in cookers provided no new circuit is required; connecting equipment to existing isolators such as electric gates or garage doors via plug and socket or fixed connection; and prefabricated modular wiring systems using plug and socket connectors.

# Part P Electrical Safety – BS 7671 Design Standards – Chunk 3

Prefabricated modular wiring systems using plug and socket connectors do not require notification to building control, though all electrical work must comply with BS 7671 standards regardless of notification status. **Duty Holders:** Designers, Installers, Electricians

All electrical installations must be designed and installed in accordance with BS 7671:2008 incorporating Amendment No 1:2011 (IET Wiring Regulations 17th Edition). Key requirements include providing sufficient information for safe operation, maintenance, and alteration through certificates, labels, and operating instructions.

Wall-mounted socket-outlets and switches in new dwellings should be installed between 450mm and 1200mm from finished floor level to comply with Part M accessibility standards. Consumer units in new dwellings should be mounted so switches are between 1350mm and 1450mm above floor level. When altering existing installations, only the new work must meet current standards unless it adversely affects existing safety or the existing installation cannot safely support the new work.

Existing installations being extended must be checked to ensure equipment rating and condition are suitable for additional loads, adequate protective measures are in place, and earthing and equipotential bonding arrangements are satisfactory. Existing cables need not be replaced solely due to old colour codes if they are otherwise adequate for continued use.

# Part P Electrical Safety – Certification Procedures – Chunk 4

Existing cables need not be replaced solely due to old colour codes if they are otherwise adequate for continued use, but all notifiable work must follow specific certification procedures. **Duty Holders:** Registered Competent Persons, Third-Party Certifiers, Installers

For notifiable work, one of three procedures must be used. Self-Certification by a Registered Competent Person involves the installer (registered with a Part P scheme) completing a BS 7671 Electrical Installation Certificate for the person ordering the work. Within 30 days, the registration body gives a Building Regulations compliance certificate to the occupier and building control body.

Third-Party Certification applies to installers who are not registered competent persons. Before work begins, they must appoint a registered third-party certifier. Within 5 days of completion, the installer notifies the certifier who inspects and tests the work. If satisfactory, the certifier issues an Electrical Installation Condition Report to the person ordering work. Within 30 days of a satisfactory report, the registration body gives a Building Regulations compliance certificate to the occupier and building control body.

Building Control Body Certification is for installers who are not registered and have not appointed a third-party certifier. Before work begins, notify the building control body. The body determines the extent of inspection and testing required. The installer provides a BS 7671 certificate and evidence of qualifications to reduce inspection charges. Upon satisfactory completion, the building control body issues a completion certificate (local authority) or final certificate (approved inspector).

# Part P Electrical Safety – Compliance and Key Terms – Chunk 5

Upon satisfactory completion of Building Control Body Certification, the building control body issues a completion certificate (local authority) or final certificate (approved inspector). **Duty Holders:** Building Control Bodies, Local Authorities, Installers

Non-notifiable work should still be designed, installed, inspected, tested, and certificated in accordance with BS 7671, though local authorities may take enforcement action if they subsequently find the work unsafe.

**Key Terms:**
- **Building control body:** Local authority or approved inspector
- **Extra-low voltage:** Not exceeding 50V AC or 120V ripple-free DC  
- **Low voltage:** Exceeding extra-low voltage but not exceeding 1000V AC or 1500V DC between conductors (or 600V AC/900V DC to earth)
- **Registered competent person:** Registered with a Part P self-certification scheme
- **Registered third-party certifier:** Registered with a Part P third-party certification scheme  
- **Electrical installation:** Fixed electric cables or fixed electrical equipment on the consumer's side of the electricity supply meter

**Standards Referred To:** 
BS 7671:2008+A1:2011 - Requirements for Electrical Installations (IET Wiring Regulations 17th Edition)
