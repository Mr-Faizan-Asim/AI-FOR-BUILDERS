# Electrical Safety – Dwellings (Part P)

## Summary
Part P of the Building Regulations requires that electrical installations in dwellings are designed and installed to protect persons from fire or injury. The 2013 edition reduces the range of notifiable work and allows third-party certification by registered certifiers as an alternative to building control approval.

## Legal Requirement (P1)

**Design and installation**
> Reasonable provision shall be made in the design and installation of electrical installations in order to protect persons operating, maintaining or altering the installations from fire or injury.

**Limits on application:** Applies only to electrical installations intended to operate at low or extra-low voltage that are:
- In or attached to a dwelling
- In the common parts of a building serving one or more dwellings (excluding power supplies to lifts)
- In a building that receives its electricity from a source located within or shared with a dwelling
- In a garden or on land associated with a dwelling where electricity is from a source within or shared with the dwelling

## Scope of Part P

**Covered:**
- Dwelling-houses, flats, and attached installations (external lighting, air conditioning, PV panels, garden electrics)
- Outbuildings (sheds, detached garages, domestic greenhouses) sharing electricity with a dwelling
- Common access areas of blocks of flats (corridors, staircases)
- Shared amenities (laundries, kitchens, gymnasiums)
- Business premises connected to the same meter as a dwelling (e.g., shop below a flat)

**Not Covered:**
- Business premises with separate metering from dwellings
- Power supplies to lifts in blocks of flats (though lift installations in single dwellings are covered)

## Notifiable Work

Building notice or full plans required for:
1. Installation of a **new circuit**
2. **Replacement of a consumer unit**
3. **Additions or alterations to existing circuits in a special location**

**Special locations** include:
- **Rooms containing a bath or shower:** The space extending vertically from floor level to 2.25m (or height of shower head if higher), and horizontally 0.6m from bath/shower tray edge (or 1.2m from center of shower head if no tray)
- **Rooms containing a swimming pool or sauna heater**

*Note: Socket-outlets should not be located within 3m of a bath tub or shower tray.*

## Non-Notifiable Work

The following do **not** require building notice (but must still comply with BS 7671):
- Additions and alterations to existing installations **outside** special locations
- Replacements, repairs, and maintenance anywhere
- Installing fixed electrical equipment (e.g., built-in cooker) provided no new circuit is required
- Connecting equipment to existing isolators (e.g., electric gates, garage doors) via plug and socket or fixed connection
- Prefabricated modular wiring systems (e.g., kitchen lighting) using plug and socket connectors

## Design and Installation Standards

**Primary Standard:** All electrical installations must be designed and installed in accordance with **BS 7671:2008 incorporating Amendment No 1:2011** (IET Wiring Regulations 17th Edition).

**Key Requirements:**
- Provide sufficient information for safe operation, maintenance, and alteration (certificates, labels, operating instructions)
- Wall-mounted socket-outlets and switches in **new dwellings** should be installed between **450mm and 1200mm** from finished floor level (Part M compliance)
- Consumer units in new dwellings should be mounted so switches are between **1350mm and 1450mm** above floor level
- When altering existing installations, only the new work must meet current standards unless it adversely affects existing safety or the existing installation cannot safely support the new work

**Additions to Existing Installations:**
Existing installations being extended must be checked to ensure:
- Equipment rating and condition are suitable for additional loads
- Adequate protective measures are in place
- Earthing and equipotential bonding arrangements are satisfactory
- Existing cables need not be replaced solely due to old colour codes if they are otherwise adequate

## Certification Procedures

For **notifiable work**, one of three procedures must be used:

### 1. Self-Certification (Registered Competent Person)
- Completed by installer registered with a Part P competent person scheme
- Installer completes BS 7671 Electrical Installation Certificate
- Certificate given to person ordering the work
- Within 30 days: Registration body gives Building Regulations compliance certificate to occupier and building control body

### 2. Third-Party Certification
- For installers who are **not** registered competent persons
- Before work begins, appoint a registered third-party certifier
- Within 5 days of completion, installer notifies certifier who inspects/tests work
- If satisfactory, certifier issues Electrical Installation Condition Report to person ordering work
- Within 30 days of satisfactory report: Registration body gives Building Regulations compliance certificate to occupier and building control body

### 3. Building Control Body Certification
- For installers who are not registered and have not appointed a third-party certifier
- Before work begins, notify building control body
- Body determines extent of inspection/testing required
- Installer provides BS 7671 certificate and evidence of qualifications to reduce inspection charges
- Upon satisfactory completion, building control body issues completion certificate (local authority) or final certificate (approved inspector)

**Non-notifiable work** should still be designed, installed, inspected, tested, and certificated in accordance with BS 7671, though local authorities may take enforcement action if they find it unsafe.

## Key Terms

- **Building control body:** Local authority or approved inspector
- **Extra-low voltage:** Not exceeding 50V AC or 120V ripple-free DC
- **Low voltage:** Exceeding extra-low voltage but not exceeding 1000V AC or 1500V DC between conductors (or 600V AC/900V DC to earth)
- **Registered competent person:** Registered with a Part P self-certification scheme
- **Registered third-party certifier:** Registered with a Part P third-party certification scheme
- **Electrical installation:** Fixed electric cables or fixed electrical equipment on the consumer's side of the electricity supply meter
